KisanMitra Canonical Agronomy Knowledge — Karnataka Bulletins, Volume 1
Compiled from the KisanMitra curated advisory set (backend/services/rag.py
offline table) so retrieval and offline fallback can never disagree.
Coverage: tomato, potato, onion, brinjal, maize, cabbage, ragi — Kolar and
Chikkaballapur belt.

BULLETIN 1 — Tomato: Basal NPK and Fertilizer Schedule (Kolar/Chikkaballapur)
Tomato is a heavy feeder. Apply balanced nutrients at 60 kg N, 50 kg P and
50 kg K per acre. Incorporate 10 tonnes of well-decomposed farmyard manure
(FYM) during basal land preparation. Split the nitrogen into three equal
doses at 0, 30 and 50 days after transplanting. For fruit setting, spray
Boron at 1 g per litre of water. For leaf spot and late blight (angamari),
spray Cymoxanil + Mancozeb at 2 g/L. Calcium or Boron deficiency causes
flower drop and blossom-end rot.

BULLETIN 2 — Tomato: Leaf Curl Virus Management
Leaf Curl Virus in Tomato causes downward curling and yellowing of leaves
and stunted growth. The disease is transmitted by whitefly. Spray
Imidacloprid at 0.3 ml per litre to control the whitefly vector. Maintain a
5 metre distance between plots for ventilation. Remove and destroy infected
plants early. Where stunting with curling appears, also foliar spray a
Micronutrient Mixture containing Zinc, Boron and Iron at 2.5 g/L every
14 days, and eliminate whitefly with Acetamiprid 0.5 g/L or Imidacloprid
0.5 ml/L.

BULLETIN 3 — Tomato and Potato: Stem Rot (Thundu Roga)
Soil-borne Sclerotium rolfsii fungus causes collar rot at the stem base;
the stem decays and the plant breaks and collapses. Drench the stem base
with Carbendazim 50 WP at 1 g/L or Ciproconazole/Tebuconazole at 1 ml/L.
Apply Trichoderma harzianum at 2 kg per acre mixed with fertilizer. Prevent
water stagnation in the field and immediately uproot and destroy infected
plants.

BULLETIN 4 — Capsicum and Tomato: Bacterial Wilt and Drenching Volume
Bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum) causes sudden drying of green
plants. Drench the stem base with Copper Oxychloride 3 g/L plus
Streptocycline 0.5 g per 10 L of water, ensuring complete root-zone
saturation of 150-200 ml of prepared solution per plant. Three days after
drenching, fertigate 19:19:19 at 3 kg per acre through drip to stimulate
fresh root growth. Keep fields free of waterlogging.

BULLETIN 5 — Brinjal and Capsicum: Root-Knot Nematode (Gantu Hula)
Soil nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.) form knots on roots and turn plants
yellow. Apply the bio-nematicide Purpureocillium lilacinus (formerly
Paecilomyces lilacinus) at 2 kg per acre, or drench Fluopyram at 1 ml/L to
the roots. Intercrop Marigold (Chenduhoo, Tagetes erecta) between rows:
marigold roots release alpha-terthienyl compounds that reduce nematode
populations, and their flowers attract Helicoverpa moths away from the
crop, acting as a trap crop.

BULLETIN 6 — Potato: Tuber Bulking and Potash Management
Potassium is critical during the tuber bulking stage at 45-60 days. Spray
Sulphate of Potash (SOP 0:0:50) at 5 g/L or 13:0:45 at 10 g/L on the 45th
and 60th day after planting. Apply MOP at 30 kg per acre to the soil at
earthing-up and irrigate lightly. Avoid excess nitrogen after day 45.

BULLETIN 7 — Onion: Thrips, Purple Blotch and Neck Rot
Onion thrips scrape the leaves and neck rot damages the bulb. Spray
Fipronil 5 SC at 2 ml/L or Spinetoram at 0.9 ml/L for thrips. For purple
blotch and downy mildew in humidity, spray Mancozeb + Carbendazim (Saaf)
at 2 g/L weekly, or Metalaxyl 8 + Mancozeb 64 WP at 2.5 g/L at 10-day
intervals. Because onion leaves are waxy, add a sticker/spreader at
0.5 ml/L to every spray. Apply Copper Oxychloride 3 g/L fifteen days
before harvest against neck rot, and stop irrigation 10-15 days before
harvest to protect storage life.

BULLETIN 8 — Maize: Nutrition and Fall Armyworm Control
At sowing apply DAP at 50 kg per acre, then Urea at 35 kg per acre on the
30th and 60th day. Fall armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) caterpillars feed
inside the whorl. Spray Emamectin Benzoate at 0.4 g/L or Chlorantraniliprole
18.5 SC at 0.4 ml/L directly into the whorl, or use Bacillus thuringiensis
(Bt) at 2 g/L. Spray early morning or late evening when caterpillars feed
actively. To slow resistance, rotate modes of action between Spinetoram,
Emamectin Benzoate and biological agents.

BULLETIN 9 — Crop Rotation and Soil Nitrogen Restoration
After cereal crops such as maize or ragi, grow legumes (groundnut,
cowpea, alasande/horse gram). Legume root nodules with Rhizobium bacteria
fix atmospheric nitrogen and restore 15-25 kg of nitrogen per acre to the
soil naturally. Treat legume seed with Rhizobium culture at 200 grams per
acre before sowing. Rotate post-Kharif maize with groundnut to break pest
cycles as well.

BULLETIN 10 — Alkaline Soil (pH 7.8): Zinc and Fertigation
Alkaline soils lock up Zinc and Iron. Apply chelated Zinc (Zn-EDTA) at
1 g/L as foliar spray, or fertigate Zinc Sulphate at 5 kg per acre
together with elemental sulphur. Do not mix Zinc Sulphate directly with
DAP or 19:19:19 in the fertigation tank, because precipitation blocks the
drip lines.

BULLETIN 11 — Cabbage: NPK Basal, Fertigation and Diamondback Moth
Cabbage needs high nitrogen and potassium for head formation. Apply FYM
10 tonnes per acre plus 60:50:50 kg NPK per acre in the basal dose.
Fertigate 19:19:19 at 3 kg per acre weekly during weeks 2-5 (vegetative)
and 13:0:45 at 4 kg per acre weekly during weeks 6-9 (head initiation).
Apply Boron 1 kg per acre and Calcium Nitrate 5 kg per acre at day 35 to
prevent hollow stem. For diamondback moth spray Chlorantraniliprole
0.4 ml/L or Bt 2 g/L. Keep spacing 45x30 cm and avoid over-irrigation at
head formation.

BULLETIN 12 — Ragi: Blast Management and Grain Fill
Neck blast and finger blast of ragi (finger millet) damage grain fill when
the panicle emerges. Maintain field sanitation and balanced potash for
strong culm. Seed treatment with Trichoderma at 4 g per kg of seed
protects the root system.
